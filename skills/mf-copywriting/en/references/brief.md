# Writing brief

Identify intended use from the request and context. If unclear, ask what the
writing will be used for, then route through [formats](formats.md). Do not run
questionnaires for every format.

## Context that matters

- Intended channel and deliverable.
- Reader, their needs, and situation.
- Purpose and main message.
- Available source material or draft.
- Output language. Follow an explicit choice; otherwise use audience and source
  context, asking only if the distinction changes the outcome.

Reuse answers already given. Ask relevant follow-ups from the selected guide in
easy-to-answer groups. Explore voice, length, action, and structure only as needed.
Age or generation can help but does not define a reader. Gen Z does not imply
slang; older readers do not automatically need formal prose. In business copy,
buying role may matter more than age. Preserve an already specified voice.

## Structure follows context

Use a requested framework. Otherwise choose an appropriate structure without
requiring the user to know or approve framework names. If they want options:

- AIDA: attention, interest, desire, action.
- PAS: problem, consequences, solution. Consequences must fit the evidence.
- Before-after-bridge: current state, desired state, route between them. Distinguish
  measured outcomes from hopes or illustrations.
- Story: situation, event or choice, consequence.
- Direct explanation: information ordered by reader need.

Stage names need not appear in final copy. Structure does not determine intimacy
or forms of address. Explain material conflicts with explicit requests.

## Start condition

Draft once intended use, reader, purpose, message, and necessary source facts are
known. Make ordinary editorial choices yourself. Generation, framework, and word
count are not universal prerequisites. Ask and wait if essential facts, intent,
or promises remain unclear. Silence is not an answer. Simple revisions preserve
source context without restarting an interview. Summarize a multi-turn brief
when helpful; no extra approval is needed once sufficient. Brief-only requests
stop at the brief. Audits use existing context rather than a new-copy interview.

For intended feel, use requested impressions and liked/disliked samples with
reasons when available: warm but firm, or confident without overstatement.
Voice samples are not mandatory for every task.


## When experience or sources are missing

For articles or content on the user's behalf, lack of firsthand experience is
not a reason to refuse or leave the task suspended. If the topic is clear but
sources are missing, find relevant sources with available research tools.
A request for a source-based article includes source research; do not request
permission again when already authorized. If access fails or research is forbidden,
explain the limit and offer user-supplied material or an angle that avoids the
unsupported claim. General explanations still need support for factual claims.

Before drafting on the user's behalf, hold a short source discussion:

- Share source links, supported findings, and evidence limits. Distinguish source
  statements from agent interpretations; read sources before citing them.
- Invite the user's response: what seems useful, questionable, or worth making the
  main message? A few possible angles can help them choose.
- Wait before attributing a personal opinion. Reuse views already supplied. If the
  user delegates angle selection, proceed with an attributed explanation and
  editorial conclusion; do not call it their personal belief or experience.

Once the direction is clear, write the content with citations near the relevant
claims. Sources support facts, not claims that the user experienced or endorsed
something. Use this discussion when evidence or personal direction is missing;
do not impose it on simple transactional copy or an already complete brief.
