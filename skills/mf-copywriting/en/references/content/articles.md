# Articles and ebooks

Clarify reader questions, perspective, knowledge, and publication context. Is this
a tutorial, opinion, explanation, or report? Use supplied sources and notes. Ask
about length, scope, or SEO only when consequential. Missing facts require sources
or verification, not fabricated experts or statistics.

Develop the reader's question with reasoned opinions and helpful examples. For
practical guides, when the material supports it, build one followable workflow:
starting material, instruction or action, then how to check the result. Merge
sections that repeat the same work. Do not force this shape on opinion, report, or
other formats with a different purpose. Let headings follow content rather than a
fixed introduction-benefits-challenges-summary sequence. Voice can come from
emphasis and perspective without first-person pronouns. Preserve attribution and
distinguish author inference from source claims.

## Practical ebooks with a conversational voice

Set learning outcomes and chapter order around reader needs. Build on earlier
knowledge without repeating the same openings, stories, and invitations. Length
follows depth of material rather than a page target or number of stories.

When the brief calls for it, use [persuasive storytelling](../language.md#conversational-copywriting-and-persuasive-storytelling).
One possible chapter flow: recognizable problem, brief story or case, lesson,
practical steps and examples, then a small exercise or result check. Choose only
parts that serve the chapter; this is an option, not a mandatory template.
Technical reference chapters may go straight to explanations and steps. Stories
should clarify the material and lead to something readers can apply.

## Final check

Check relevance, unnecessary repetition, and evidence-proportional conclusions.
An opening alone should not cram every limitation into its first paragraphs.
