# Skill instruction language

Use for SKILL.md and references read by agents, including mf-copywriting itself.
Readers are executing agents and people reviewing instructions.

Use established purpose, triggers, workflow, and boundaries. Language edits must
preserve scope and behavior. Ask about actual ambiguity rather than quietly making
new decisions. Follow environment skill-creation and registry rules for packaging;
this guide governs wording rather than replacing those rules.

- State actions and objects directly: "Read previous findings", not "Perform the
  execution of an examination of previous findings".
- Name specific triggers and subjects: language usage, communication style, and
  copywriting patterns rather than ambiguous "skill trends".
- Use natural, direct prose without forced slang. Retain familiar technical terms
  when more precise than a strained translation; explain unfamiliar ones as needed.
- Distinguish requirements, options, and examples. Preserve must, only, may, and
  stop conditions without weakening or strengthening their meaning.
- Specify useful outcomes and completion conditions. Examples clarify rules rather
  than introduce hidden requirements.
- Link references clearly, avoid duplicated long rules, and write invocation
  descriptions as triggers rather than promotional capability claims.

Before delivery, check actions, objects, conditions, outcomes, authorization,
prohibitions, meaningful ordering, numbers, paths, commands, and tool names against
the source. Natural wording must preserve operational meaning. Skill documents
address agents; audience-only public-copy rules do not remove operational
instructions from agent documentation.
