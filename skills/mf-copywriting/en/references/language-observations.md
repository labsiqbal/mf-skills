# Current language observations

Read when the brief requests current trends, slang, or platform/generation-specific
usage.

For a refresh, follow [mf-copywriting-update](update.md). For writing, consult
[current findings](current-findings.md) for the brief's context. This file defines
evidence rules; the findings document records actual research status.

## Research

Specify country/community, language, platform, time window, and audience context.
English does not imply the United States. Distinguish national, regional, and
community usage. Cover Gen Alpha, Gen Z, millennials, and boomers. Generational labels require evidence; do not infer commenters'
ages from slang or profiles alone.

Use relevant public sources such as recent TikTok/YouTube comments and surrounding
posts/conversations. Sample across creators and topics; one community is not a
whole country. Comments can be irony, quotations, spam, or fandom language. One
viral video does not establish broad popularity.

Record each finding with:

- Expression/pattern, meaning, function, and a short contextual example.
- Country/community, language, platform, and relationship context.
- Source URLs, content dates, and retrieval/review dates.
- Sampling method, inspected sample size, and coverage limits.
- Cross-source evidence, uncertainty, and unsuitable contexts.
- Status: observed, needs confirmation, or needs refresh.

Retain only necessary quotations, omitting unnecessary identities or personal data.
Collection tools must report failures and access limits; missing data is not a
finding. Do not build a custom scraper before sources and access needs are known.

## Use in copy

Select only findings that fit the brief and voice. Generational slang is not a
prerequisite for personal writing. Comment language is not automatically suitable
for brands, captions, landing pages, or client communications. Check meaning,
possible misreading, and current use in the intended context.

Without relevant recent evidence, do not claim an expression is trending. Use
natural language independent of trends, or request examples/research as needed.
Recheck relevant sources before treating old observations as current; expressions
have no universal expiry period.
