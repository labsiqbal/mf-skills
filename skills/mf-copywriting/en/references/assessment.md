# Indonesian and English writing assessment

For judgments about whether copy is good or worth selecting, use
[taste assessment](taste.md) first. The matrix below diagnoses language/form
issues when needed; do not show both matrices by default. Check facts and
requirements separately. These are editorial aids, not AI authorship detectors
or scientific quality measurements. Use the [brief](brief.md) for
reader, writer, purpose, language, format, intimacy, and structure. Mark voice
judgments provisional without samples. Formal and conversational prose can both fit.

Score each dimension 1-10 using the bands in [taste assessment](taste.md).
Use the anchors below for language/form diagnosis. High scores require positive
evidence rather than counting particular words.

| Dimension | 1 - Rewrite | 9-10 - Strong in context |
|---|---|---|
| Sentence fluency | Translation-like syntax or unclear references | Idiomatic target-language syntax and clear focus |
| Vocabulary and address | Wrong register, forced slang, or inaccurate terms | Precise words and consistent relationship-appropriate address |
| Voice and connection | Interchangeable filler; missing relevant perspective | Grounded detail and choices reveal appropriate care without invention |
| Clarity and specificity | Readers must guess; abstraction substitutes for explanation | Information and examples make meaning and implications clear |
| Flow and rhythm | Repetition, abrupt jumps, or dramatic fragments obstruct | Ideas develop with useful links and content-led sentence length |
| Purpose and format | Ignores situation, visual, relationship, or needed action | Information, length, and structure serve the actual reading context |

Do not require humor, opinion, or personal stories in notices and transactions.
Mark a dimension unassessable with reasons if evidence is insufficient. Each score
requires a short quotation and contextual reason. "Feels AI" is not evidence.
For 1-4, suggest a meaning-preserving edit; identify missing facts instead of inventing.

Check facts and requirements separately: numbers, dates, quotes, conditions,
uncertainty, promises, length, output language, and separation of audience copy
from internal notes. Mark each compliant, needs revision, or unverifiable. Without
sources, do not call claims verified. Style scores never excuse factual errors.
"We will update you tomorrow" must not become "It ships tomorrow."

When assessment is requested, deliver a compact dimension/score/quote-and-reason/
revision table, factual status, and highest-impact edits. Keep dimensions separate;
no total human score or percentages, and no predictions of reach or sales. Compare
versions with the same context and material, noting self-assessment and shared
conversation limits. Calibrate against specific user reactions before changing rules.

## Calibration and limits on conclusions

A 9-10 needs dimension-specific evidence, not merely an absence of errors. Clear but
generic writing does not automatically have distinctive voice. Distinguish task-fit
from resemblance to an author or brand without samples. Unsupported added details
fail the factual check even when fluent. Do not declare all requirements met after
finding a violation. Validation, one session, or self-scoring cannot establish
consistency, audience preference, or improvement caused by the skill.

These are illustrative editorial anchors, not inter-rater findings. Shared brief:
a brief WhatsApp update from a store to a customer awaiting a paid order. Shipping
is late; revised date unknown; next update tomorrow at 15:00 WIB. Warm, respectful,
no invented reason or compensation.

**A:** "With reference to the delay in shipment, an informational update will be
provided tomorrow at 15:00 WIB. The revised shipping date is not known. Kindly
await further information."

**B:** "Your order is delayed. We do not yet know the revised shipping date. We
will update you tomorrow at 15:00 WIB. Thank you for your patience."

**C:** "We're sorry your order is delayed. We don't yet have a revised shipping
date. We'll update you tomorrow at 15:00 WIB."

| Dimension | A | B | C |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fluency | 4: "With reference to" burdens a chat. | 7: repeated "We" feels mechanical. | 9: "We're sorry your order" reads naturally. |
| Words and address | 4: "Kindly await" is distant here. | 7: "your patience" is formulaic. | 9: "We'll update you" fits the relationship. |
| Voice and connection | 4: "will be provided" centers process. | 7: "Your order" is relevant, closing generic. | 9: "We're sorry" acknowledges impact; distinct brand voice remains unassessed. |
| Clarity | 7: "informational update" is indirect, timing clear. | 9: "revised shipping date" is distinct from update time. | 9: "don't yet have" preserves uncertainty with a clear next update. |
| Flow and rhythm | 4: "further information" repeats the update. | 7: repeated "We" and optional closing. | 9: apology, uncertainty, then update without repetition. |
| Purpose and format | 4: "Kindly await" makes a brief chat bureaucratic. | 7: "Thank you" can be cut for directness. | 9: three sentences provide the necessary customer update. |

All three preserve supplied facts; style explains score differences. Replacing
C's update with "We'll ship tomorrow at 15:00 WIB" fails factual fidelity. Calling
an unspecified visual "this video" also needs revision. Fluency is not fidelity.
