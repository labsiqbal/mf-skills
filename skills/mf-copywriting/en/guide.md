# mf-copywriting

Follow the output language in the brief. Give writing a voice through attention,
perspective, and meaningful detail. Personal does not require casual language.

## Workflow

1. Identify intended use. Read [brief](references/brief.md), then select relevant
   guidance through [formats](references/formats.md). Reuse known context; ask
   only consequential missing questions. Wait for answers that determine content.
   An audit does not require a new writing interview.
   When experience or sources are missing, follow [research and source discussion](references/brief.md#when-experience-or-sources-are-missing).
2. For voice, stiff wording, or cross-language adaptation, read
   [language and voice](references/language.md).
3. Choose structure after purpose and material are clear. Preserve the author's voice.
4. Review as the intended reader. Remove repetition and empty sentences. Recheck
   every required fact, number, promise, and format limit before calling the copy
   ready. Fix or flag failures; do not hide them behind smoother wording. Deliver
   final copy; explain edits or offer variants only when requested.

## Fixed boundaries

Preserve facts, numbers, quotations, conditions, meaning, and uncertainty. Never
invent experiences, testimonials, outcomes, or promises to create a personal voice.
Check descriptive modifiers and medium labels too: a visual description does not
establish video, and a notice is not necessarily long. Added factual detail needs
support; editorial reasoning can be stated clearly as opinion.
Identify illustrations as examples. Writing does not authorize publishing.
Use ordinary punctuation without em dashes.

Visible slide, page, and interface text addresses the end audience. Put production
status, builder instructions, and missing-material notes in a separate handoff.
A prototype deliverable does not automatically mean a demo product. Display
beta/demo wording only when relevant to the audience or requested. Keep product
limitations, warnings, and terms that affect user decisions. Before delivery,
inspect every visible label and button for internal notes or unfinished
placeholders. Ask for essential missing material; never fabricate it to look
finished. When another skill builds the page or deck, apply these copy boundaries
alongside its artifact and visual workflow.

For testing, read [test cases](references/tests.md). For judgments about copy quality or selection, use [taste assessment](references/taste.md).
Use [assessment](references/assessment.md) for language/form diagnosis; do not show
scores by default. For skill
instruction audits, read [skill audit](references/skill-audit.md).

When writing or editing skill instructions, including mf-copywriting itself, read
[skill instruction language](references/content/skill-instructions.md). Apply it
to update-guide edits without starting a marketing brief.
